# 3 Dataset preparation

## 3.1 Summary

Preparation followed the order of {tab:decisionlog}, whose last column gives the shape after each step, and {fig:pipeline} draws the same flow. Type-safe loading read every Accidents row as text, and scope filtering kept 101,609 in-scope reports. Integration added the Mines and previous-year production tables with asserted row counts, the error rules blanked or recoded impossible values, and the duplicate audit removed nothing. Feature engineering and the leakage audit left the features of {tab:dictionary}, and the mine-year grouped split held out a fifth of the rows for testing, without resampling. Imputation, outlier handling, encoding and scaling then turned the training split into an 81,289 x 546 matrix. All learned transformations are fitted on the training split only inside a scikit-learn Pipeline with ColumnTransformer (Pedregosa et al., 2011).

## 3.2 Integration and error correction

I read every column as text, because type inference would strip the leading zeros from MINE_ID, and asserted every join's row count ({tab:sources}). The error rules learn nothing from the data, which let them run on all rows before the split. Job experience exceeded total experience on 3,731 rows by a median of 6.5 years, too much for rounding ({tab:decisionlog}). Clipping assumes the job field is overstated and a swap assumes a data-entry exchange; I set the value to missing with a flag, because neither assumption can be checked (Han et al., 2022). A blank stops invented tenure from moving a case across the review threshold.

## 3.3 Duplicates and multi-victim accidents

No row is an exact duplicate, and five rows match only when the document number, closing document and narrative are ignored ({tab:decisionlog}). Dropping them assumes double filing; keeping them assumes distinct victims, as MSHA requires one Form 7000-1 per injured person (Mine Safety and Health Administration, no date). Collapsing the 2,614 same-day multi-victim groups would make the accident the unit, although insurers reserve per claimant. I kept every row, one per claimant to reserve, and the grouped split keeps each same-day group on one side.

## 3.4 Missing values

Numeric gaps are small except for the previous-year lost-time share, absent on 19.8% of training rows ({fig:missing}). The gaps are not missing completely at random (MCAR): blank-experience reports are lost-time in 59.3% of training cases against 48.5% of recorded ones. Blank experience clusters in early years ({tab:decisionlog}), consistent with missing at random (MAR) given year and mine (Rubin, 1976). The lost-time share is structurally undefined (zero over zero) at mines without a previous-year case.

Listwise deletion needs MCAR, mean imputation is pulled up by the right tail of skewed columns, and KNNImputer or IterativeImputer, which predict gaps from other features, would invent histories for quiet mines (van Buuren, 2018). I chose SimpleImputer with the median for skewed numerics plus seven explicit indicators, since add_indicator would repeat one flag across six co-missing exposure columns. The indicators carry the higher lost-time rate of unrecorded reports into the score, which keeps more such cases in the review queue. Structural placeholders became their own level and other categorical gaps a constant "missing" level, since most_frequent would place surface accidents underground. Columns over 60% blank, other than structural placeholders, were dropped.

## 3.5 Outliers

Total experience has a skewness of 1.37 and previous-year hours 4.27 ({tab:descriptives}). Right skew inflates the standard deviation behind the Z-score rule and puts its lower fence below zero. Tukey's IQR fences rest on quartiles (Tukey, 1977) but sit the same distance beyond each quartile, so at this skew they flag over five times as many experience rows as the Z-score rule ({fig:outliers}), all plausible veteran careers. I kept the values, added log1p versions and left clipping as an application option. Lagged rates were winsorised, since at the smallest mines a single case gives an extreme rate. Linear, distance-based and Gaussian models react to such extremes, while tree splits use only rank.

## 3.6 Instance selection and class balance

Scope filtering was the only instance selection and left a training prevalence of 48.6% ({tab:decisionlog}). SMOTE inside the folds assumes interpolated neighbours are plausible cases, which fails for one-hot codes (Chawla et al., 2002; Lemaitre et al., 2017), and under- or over-sampling discards or repeats rows. I applied no resampling, because each of these would also bias the probabilities that adjusters see and that set the cost-optimal threshold. I searched class_weight for logistic regression and left the cost asymmetry to an out-of-fold threshold tuned in Section 4. The test set was never resampled.

## 3.7 Feature engineering

Every engineered feature uses fields known at filing ({tab:dictionary}), because leakage would overstate the test recall on which review capacity is planned. The Mines attributes kept are location and commodity fields, which rarely change, and the 103(i) status re-dated to the accident. I excluded three undated flags (safety committee, Part 48 training and portable operation), because the snapshot gives no value at the accident date. Clock and calendar features give accident and shift-start hours, a night flag, hours into shift, weekday, weekend and month. Experience features add log1p of total experience, mine and job shares of the total and a new-to-mine flag. Exposure features give hours per employee and size bands, and history features turn previous-year cases filed before the accident date into rates per 200,000 hours and a lost-time share. Same-year hours were rejected because quarterly hours reports arrive after the quarter (Legal Information Institute, no date b), and accident-date counting because 1.9% of prior cases were filed after the accident ({tab:decisionlog}; Kaufman et al., 2012; Kuhn and Johnson, 2019).

## 3.8 Encoding and scaling

Most categorical features are nominal codes with up to 193 levels ({tab:decisionlog}). Ordinal encoding assumes an order that linear models read as magnitude and target encoding needs fold-safe estimates, so I chose OneHotEncoder with handle_unknown="infrequent_if_exist" and a min_frequency of 0.1%, which pools rare and unseen levels. The ordered size band stayed one-hot, as its lost-time rate is not monotonic, and only the target was label-encoded. Left unscaled, hours would dominate distances and a nearest-neighbour model's flags would follow mine size. Under MinMaxScaler a few very large mines would squeeze most previous-year hours into the bottom tenth of the range, and Normalizer mixes units within a row. I applied StandardScaler to logistic regression, K-nearest neighbours (KNN), support vector machine (SVM), multilayer perceptron (MLP) and Gaussian naive Bayes and no scaler to the tree models, whose splits ignore monotone rescaling (Pedregosa et al., 2011).

## 3.9 Train, validation and test split

A stratified random split would treat rows as independent, but the mean label of later accidents in the same mine-year ranks a row with a ROC-AUC of 0.667, near the best planned feature ({tab:decisionlog}). Four numeric features, the coordinates and previous-year hours and employees, identify the mine-year for 99.9% of rows, which would let KNN and tree ensembles memorise outcomes. Grouping by mine alone would test only unseen mines, yet 91.9% of reports from 2020 to 2025 came from mines with a report before 2020.

I held out one StratifiedGroupKFold fold of mine-year groups (random_state 42) as the test set, with five-fold StratifiedGroupKFold on the training rows for validation (Kohavi, 1995). Assertions confirm disjoint groups and prevalence within one percentage point of the whole. The insurer therefore sees recall on unseen mine-years, with temporal and mine-grouped splits as secondary checks.

Mine-year grouping leaves one path between the splits. The history features prev_incidents, prev_incident_rate_200k, prev_lost_time_rate_200k and prev_lost_time_share are built from the full Accidents file. For 11,426 of the 81,289 training rows (14.1%) the mine's previous year therefore sits in the test split, and 11,416 of these rows carry a history computed from test outcomes. The primary evaluation keeps this operational definition of the features. Section 4.5 reports two leakage-free checks: the refit with those histories blanked on the training rows, which gives a test PR-AUC of 0.842 and a recall of 0.941 at the capacity threshold, both equal to the reference fit at three decimals, and the temporal split, whose training rows use only earlier outcomes.
