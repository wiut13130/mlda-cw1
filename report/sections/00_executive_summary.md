# Executive summary

The question is whether a mine injury report is a lost-time case needing an indemnity reserve and a return-to-work plan. Reports already carrying a return-to-work date are not scored; the 55% still open at filing (open reports) are scored on receipt of Form 7000-1. Histogram-based gradient boosting (HGB) was selected from eight algorithms tuned with mine-year-grouped cross-validation on 101,609 Mine Safety and Health Administration reports of 2010-2025, joined to mine attributes, previous-year hours and case history.

Fitted on all filings, HGB has a test area under the precision-recall curve (PR-AUC) of 0.842. For open reports I recommend the workload rule: review the top three in four by score. At a cut-off of 0.376, fixed from training scores before testing, it flags 74.3% of the 11,290 open test reports and finds 0.850 of their lost-time cases; with exactly three in four reviewed, HGB reaches 0.855 against 0.828 for a lookup table of nature, body part and sector. The all-filings capacity threshold flags 76.3% of test filings with recall 0.941 but would flag 90.0% of open reports. Because the study is retrospective, operational use waits for a prospective trial on reports scored at receipt, with a model refitted on open reports (recall 0.877) as the candidate and this model as the comparator. An adjuster decides every flagged claim, and because the label also records restricted-duty practice, results are reported by sector and the score must not rank mines for inspection.
